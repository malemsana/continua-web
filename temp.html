If you're asking about India, the answer is: possibly, but don't assume you automatically own exclusive copyright in the raw AI outputs. The situation is a bit unusual because Indian copyright law explicitly recognizes a "computer-generated work" and says the author is the person who causes the work to be created. That is more favorable to your position than simply saying "AI art has no copyright in India." However, there are two separate questions: 1. Do you have the right to use the icons? This depends heavily on the AI service's terms of use. If you generated them with a service whose terms grant you rights to your outputs and don't prohibit commercial use, then you may be able to use them commercially. That's the first thing I'd verify for the particular AI tool you used. 2. Do you have exclusive copyright over the resulting artwork? That's less certain. Copyright protects original expression, and the Indian Copyright Office says copyright arises automatically for an original work. But AI-generated artwork creates a difficult authorship question: how much of the final artistic expression came from you, versus the model? Your situation is better than: "I typed one prompt and accepted the first image." You said you went through multiple iterations and presumably selected/refined the results. If you also made meaningful human edits—composition changes, cleanup, removing artifacts, modifying shapes, combining elements, etc.—your claim to copyright in those human-created contributions becomes considerably stronger. The Indian Copyright Office itself recognizes that websites can contain copyrightable artistic works such as images, diagrams and illustrations, while also noting that things like common icons/familiar symbols may not themselves qualify. For your Continua icons specifically I'd distinguish three things: The concept "Use a microscope to represent Biology." Probably not protectable by itself. Copyright doesn't protect ideas/concepts. The particular icon artwork Your specific green microscope illustration. Potentially copyrightable as an artistic work, but the strength of your exclusive claim depends on its originality/authorship and the AI tool's terms. Your customized final asset If you've substantially edited the generated image yourself, the human-created modifications can give you a much stronger copyright position in those modifications. And there's an important practical point You don't necessarily need to register copyright before using them. The Indian Copyright Office says copyright generally arises automatically and registration isn't mandatory; registration provides evidentiary advantages in disputes. So for Continua, I'd do this: Keep the original generation files/prompts/iterations. Keep records showing the iterations and your modifications. Keep the AI provider's Terms of Service applicable on the date you generated them. Keep your final edited/source files. Verify that the provider permits commercial use and doesn't impose special output restrictions. If Continua becomes commercially significant and these assets are important to the brand, consider formal copyright registration with the Indian Copyright Office. The Office currently lists registration for an artistic work at ₹500 per work, or ₹2,000 where the literary/artistic work is used or capable of being used in relation to goods or services. So I wouldn't throw the icons away or worry that they're unusable. You can very likely use them if the generator's terms allow it. The question is whether you can confidently say "Continua owns exclusive copyright in this exact artwork"—and that requires looking at the AI tool's terms and the degree of human contribution.
